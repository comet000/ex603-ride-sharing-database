# Unit 5 Finding: The Drivers to Check, and the Drivers Our Reports Missed

**To:** Leadership team  
**From:** Trust and Data team

**Bottom line:** Unit 3 corrected a dashboard that overstated rider no-shows and had led to a frozen driver bonus. The actual rate was **6.0% (12 of 200 trips)**. Unit 4 then identified drivers **2 and 3** for closer review and noted that drivers **14 and 15** were absent from its trip-based reports. Unit 5 adds a closer comparison for drivers 2 and 3 and shows why trip-based reports leave out drivers 14 and 15. We'd review the first group's trips, but check account and dispatch history for the second group.

## Who we'd investigate

Driver **2** had **3 rider no-shows on 16 trips (18.75%)**. Seven drivers with similar trip counts had **4 on 122 trips (3.28%)**. Driver **3** had **2 on 13 (15.38%)**, compared with **3 on 52 (5.77%)** among four similar-volume drivers. That reinforces the concern we raised in Unit 4: driver 2 is first on the review list, then driver 3.

Driver **4** had **1 no-show on 6 trips (16.67%)**, which looks high as a percentage but is still a single event. We'd keep watching that driver rather than treating the rate as a pattern.

Drivers 2 and 3 share the **Night Owl** badge, but three other drivers have it too, with different no-show records. None of the five no-show trips associated with drivers 2 and 3 was requested after 10 p.m. The badge doesn't explain the result.

## The drivers missing from the activity report

The join comparison confirms the earlier finding: a trip-based report shows only **13 drivers**, while the full driver list has **15**. Drivers 14 and 15 have no trips in this 2023 extract, and both are marked active. They also have stored ratings, so the absence of trips is worth checking rather than assuming they never worked.

A report that leaves them out makes it harder to see the gap. Including every registered driver, even those with zero trips, gives leadership a more complete picture of who is in the system and whose activity needs checking.

## What this doesn't tell us

A rider no-show doesn't tell us that the driver did anything wrong. We don't know from these records whether the driver reached the pickup point, how long they waited, or whether the trip was labeled correctly. Likewise, zero trips in a 2023 extract doesn't tell us whether a driver worked earlier, couldn't receive assignments, or has missing records. The account's active flag has no date showing when it last changed. These are leads to check, not conclusions about responsibility or account errors.

## Suggested next step

Have **Driver Operations and Rider Support** review the five no-show trips for drivers 2 and 3, including arrival, contact attempts, waiting time, and the recorded reason. Recheck driver 4 after more trips. Driver Operations should separately confirm whether drivers 14 and 15 were eligible to receive trips and whether their accounts are current, while **Trust and Data** checks for missing history and the source of their stored ratings. If the check finds an account or dispatch problem, resolve it. If either driver is no longer participating, update the account status. Driver 3's inactive flag is also worth confirming against its account history.

For future activity reports, include all registered drivers with zero-trip rows where appropriate. That would keep drivers like 14 and 15 visible instead of dropping them from leadership's view.
